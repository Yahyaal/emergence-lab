# Literature: prior work narrows our novelty claims

<p class="lead">This review was done by AI agents (Claude) on 16 November 2026: about 190 searches and page reads across six research passes, many sources read through a summarising fetch tool. No human expert has checked it. It is not exhaustive. "Not found in this search" means only that our searches did not find it; it never means "new".</p>

The six working notes are in `docs/data/literature/`: [program soups and replicators]({{ '/data/literature/program_soups_and_replicators.md' | relative_url }}), [after replicators: open-ended evolution]({{ '/data/literature/after_replicators_open_ended_evolution.md' | relative_url }}), [Lenia and starting conditions]({{ '/data/literature/lenia_and_starting_conditions.md' | relative_url }}), [search methods and validation]({{ '/data/literature/search_methods_and_validation.md' | relative_url }}), and two gap-closing passes, [replicators and evolution]({{ '/data/literature/gaps_replicators_and_evolution.md' | relative_url }}) and [Lenia and cellular automata]({{ '/data/literature/gaps_lenia_and_cellular_automata.md' | relative_url }}). The [Paper]({{ '/paper.html' | relative_url }}) was revised with this review (J-091, D-066).

## Summary

The literature we searched contradicts none of the core findings in the [Paper]({{ '/paper.html' | relative_url }}). It does change how much of them can be called new.

- **Two results are already published:** replicators arise in random BFF soups, and plain SUBLEQ and RSUBLEQ4 make none ([Agüera y Arcas et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19108)).
- **Most others are partly known.** Reverse-copying and self-inverting replicators, the importance of head start positions, heads 64 apart, timing emergence per interaction, and heredity-free division in Lenia variants all have published or public-code precedents.
- **Two groups of results were not found in this search,** which is weaker than "new": the quantified Lenia contrast between smooth, shuffled and soup starts at matched mass, and the rule-level F predictor with its designed SUBLEQ family.
- **Several results are in tension with published work,** though none in direct conflict. The worst case was our own wording: "We could not reproduce movers (E-16)" turned a result that is not comparable with Flow-Lenia's into an apparent failed reproduction. The SUBLEQ "single pair run writes the founder" result would conflict with published work if applied to BFF. The 1-in-1,000 multi-channel divider sits against Chan's "not rare".
- **The negatives after emergence** (no fitness gain, no parasites, no effect of space) are mostly what the mechanisms behind Tierra and spatial-replicator theory predict. They are reasonable measurements but not surprising.
- **By field standards,** our soups and lattices are the right size for BFF. Replication is far too low, our instruments cannot see the main kinds of adaptation the theory predicts, and the 45 pre-registered primaries still lack a multiple-comparison correction.

The review listed 22 citations and rewordings the paper needed before it could claim anything beyond "consistent with and extending" this literature. Most are applied in this change (see [Corrections](#corrections-to-our-documents)).

## How the labels and reading depths were assigned

This page draws on six research-note files written on 16 November 2026. Four are first-pass notes (program soups, post-emergence evolution, Lenia and starting conditions, search and validation methods). Two are later gap-closing notes that re-read several sources at greater depth. Where the gap notes correct the first pass, we follow the gap notes. The main corrections were these:

- Knierim et al. 2026 and Cicala et al. 2026 were read in full, not through a summary page or abstract.
- Lenski et al. 2003's reward-all condition evolved EQU in **23 of 50** populations (46%), not "a bit over a third".
- Bedau, Snyder and Packard 1998 examined only Echo, its neutral shadow and the fossil record. They did not classify Tierra.
- The "sloppy copiers at 59 to 60 of 64 bytes" were in variant BFF rules, not standard BFF. The first pass's inference that standard BFF sits in the "survival of the flattest" regime is therefore unsupported.
- Flow-Lenia's single-channel optimisation for directed motion did not simply fail. It succeeded in longer runs.

We use four labels.

- **Published:** a source read at full-text or code level reports the same result.
- **Partly known:** the phenomenon or idea is published, but our specific measurement, control or explanation was not found.
- **Not found in this search:** the queries listed in the notes turned up nothing. This is **not** a claim of novelty: several key primary sources were closed, and Google Scholar was never queried.
- **Tension:** a published statement points the other way, or would do so if our claim were read broadly.

One claim (A8) gets a fifth description, **not comparable**: the two results measure different things, so neither confirms nor contradicts the other (D-066).

Reading depth is given as **FULL** (full text, sometimes through a summarising fetch tool), **CODE** (repository source), **ABS** (abstract or metadata only), **SEC** (search summary or third-party page) and **REPO** (our own files). Sources that the notes mark as from memory or unverified are not described in detail here: Eigen 1971, Zaman et al. 2014, Czárán and Szathmáry 2000, Hurlbert 1984, Lehman et al. 2020, Langton 1990, Maynard Smith and Szathmáry, and Lenski 1991. Numbers attributed to Coreworld and to Pargellis's Amoeba come only from secondary accounts and are not repeated here.

## Program soups: two results published, most refined, one overreach

**The BFF reproduction (B1)** is correctly framed as a reproduction. It is also more consistent with the source paper than the paper's §4.1 first said ("We did not compare rates with theirs"). The review did the arithmetic. At our pooled rate of **0.036 per 10⁸ pair runs**, Agüera y Arcas et al.'s 16k epochs at 2^17 programs give an expected 0.38 events, so P(transition) ≈ 0.32, against their reported **40%** ([Agüera y Arcas et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19108)). Our background mutation rate, **0.00024 per byte per round**, equals the source paper's 0.024% (`emergence/programs/bff.py`, REPO). Two caveats remain. It is unverified whether the source paper's soups used `bff_noheads`, where both heads start at byte 0 like our standard rule, or `bff`, where the heads are read from the program's first two bytes ([cubff bff.inc.h](https://github.com/paradigms-of-intelligence/cubff/blob/main/bff.inc.h)). And the arithmetic extrapolates our rate to 2^17 programs, beyond the largest 64-byte soups we measured (2^16). This is arithmetic done in the review, not a result of ours. A cubff user report of failed or very slow emergence at 1,024 programs also fits the size dependence we measured ([cubff issue #46](https://github.com/paradigms-of-intelligence/cubff/issues/46)).

**The size-scaling and one-rate claims (B2, B10)** are partly known. Knierim et al. measure time to the first replicator as **about 2.5 × 10⁶ interactions**, averaged over 100 seeds. They say different soup sizes gave "very similar results", which is the same per-interaction model we fit. They report means only: no histogram, no exponential fit and no size analysis ([Knierim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01483)). The cubff repository holds a fresh-pair spawn-rate estimator and a mean-residual-life test of memorylessness, dated 2025, with no published results ([cubff PR #34](https://github.com/paradigms-of-intelligence/cubff/pull/34); [cubff PR #29](https://github.com/paradigms-of-intelligence/cubff/pull/29)). Our explicit size sweep with a pre-registered exponent was not found anywhere, which makes it the defensible part. Knierim's number must not be used as a check on our rate without two caveats. It is for `bff_selfmove`, a variant designed to make copying easier. And it counts first appearance, not take-over; the source paper's seeded runs suggest only about one in five replicators takes over. The two rates differ by about 1,000-fold. Knierim's text also disagrees with its own Table 1: the text says CUST64 is "roughly a factor of 25" better than the soup, but the table ratio is about 53. So we quote the table, not the text's factors.

**The backward-copying story (B3, B4)** splits in two. The observation is published. The source paper's case-study replicator is a palindrome that copies correctly "despite being copied in reverse". A cubff pull request reports "palindromic self-replicators in bff ... with varying inter-head distances". Knierim et al. note that "some self-replicators invert themselves" and need an even number of executions, which is why their detector uses an odd chain ([Agüera y Arcas et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19108); [cubff PR #14](https://github.com/paradigms-of-intelligence/cubff/pull/14); [Knierim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01483)). Head positions are also a known lever. The source paper found that "adding an offset to head1 is sufficient to allow looping self-replicators to arise" on long tapes. Knierim et al. deliberately favour byte 64 so that heads start "exactly 64 positions apart", cutting programs tested per replicator from 4.5 × 10⁵ to 9.4 × 10⁴. What was not found is our explanation and test: a kernel census showing that with both heads at byte 0 all 55 short copiers run in reverse, and the single-change soup counterfactual (8 of 8 against 0 of 12) with an h = 127 control that separates "more copiers" from "forward copiers". The source paper's v1 and v2 were not compared, which should be checked before we claim the census explanation.

**The F predictor (B5) and the designed SUBLEQ family (B8)** are the strongest "not found in this search" results on the program side. The search was broad. It covered AlChemy and its 2024 revisit, Squirm3, Stringmol, Kruszewski and Mikolov's combinator chemistry, Avida's exhaustive replicator counts and 29 papers citing the source paper. **None tests a rule-level predictor prospectively across language variants** ([Mathis et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.12137); [Hutton 2002](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/bio_sim/articles/hutton_rep_molecules.pdf); [Hickinbotham et al. 2021](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/176868/1/rsos.210441.pdf); [Kruszewski and Mikolov](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08245)). F still has ancestors that we now cite. The source paper proposed shortest replicator length as "a key factor" but "not the sole factor". Adami's information-theoretic estimate gives the chance of a random replicator as D^−I, and C G et al. found **914 replicators among all 26⁸ length-8 Avida genomes** by exhaustive enumeration, close in method to our kernel census ([Adami 2015](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1409.0590); [C G et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1701.03993)). Replicator density among random programs is the obvious baseline that F was never tested against. The SUBLEQ periodic tiles have precedents in other languages. The source paper's 8080 soups made only non-looping two-byte repeats. Coreworld's two-instruction MOV-SPL "often take[s] over" (as described in the source paper). AlChemy collapses to the identity function. These all show that trivial copiers are the default attractor wherever a language allows them. R_P has an unpublished twin in cubff's `selfrep_spawning.py`, which pairs a program with fresh random partners and counts the replicators that result ([cubff PR #34](https://github.com/paradigms-of-intelligence/cubff/pull/34)).

**The overreach was B9.** Phase 5 of E-75 shows that **SUBLEQ** founders were written in one pair run (24 by the partner, 16 by the program itself, 0 by mutation alone). But the paper's summary of Finding B made this the general picture: "replicators appear when a rare pair run writes one, so anything that raises the number of pair runs ... brings the jump sooner". For BFF that conflicts with two published results. The source paper's tracer follows its case-study origin through **five stages over several interactions**: a pre-replicator, a reversed replicator, a direct replicator, a blocked copy, then completion. Knierim et al. find BFF replicators faster by mutation-only random walks under tuned byte distributions than the soup finds them. They also find that replicators still arise when no copying between programs is allowed ([Agüera y Arcas et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19108); [Knierim et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01483)). One nuance matters: with uniform random bytes, mutation-only walks are *slower* than the soup (9.5 × 10⁸ against 5 × 10⁶ programs tested at p = 1/200). So "mutation suffices" depends on the sampling distribution. Either way, the single-run origin claim is now limited to SUBLEQ (D-066). Our own data already show that the exponential model is only approximate (8 of 26 rules in the 5% tails, p = 0.003).

**The pre-2024 history** of spontaneous digital replicators was missing from the paper. Coreworld (Rasmussen et al. 1990), Pargellis's Amoeba (1996 to 2003), Hutton's Squirm3 (2002) and Greenbaum and Pargellis (2017) all report replicators arising from random code or atoms. LaBar, Hintze and Adami (2016) studied how such spontaneously arising Avida replicators evolve afterwards ([LaBar et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.07959); [Greenbaum and Pargellis 2017 record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1162%2FARTL_A_00234)). The full texts of Coreworld and Pargellis were closed (HTTP 403 or paywalled), so their rates and any waiting-time data are unverified. We cite them as prior demonstrations, not for numbers.

## Lenia: the shuffle control survives, the Flow-Lenia sentence did not

**Finding A's central claim (A2),** that spatial correlation of the start causes movers, holds up best of our spatial results. Starting from correlated or compact states is established practice: Chan's patches, Flow-Lenia's centred 40×40 patch, Reinke et al.'s CPPN starts, MaCE's Perlin circle and the phase-boundaries paper's whole-grid Perlin noise. Reinke et al. say outright that per-cell white noise "tend[s] to evolve into dead or global patterns" ([Reinke et al. project page](http://developmentalsystems.org/intrinsically_motivated_discovery_of_diverse_patterns)). The phase-boundaries authors write that they are "implicitly selecting for interesting dynamics" with Perlin starts, and they list varying the initial condition as future work ([Papadopoulos et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17848v2)). Hudcová et al. vary only the area of a uniform-noise patch, from 10² to 90², over more than 10⁵ systems ([Hudcová et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01932)). Gray-Scott work shows that spot outcomes depend on the size of the initial perturbation, but not as a rate study (SEC only). **No work found sweeps correlation length at fixed mass or uses a value-preserving shuffle.** So A2 is "not found in this search", with a qualitative motivation that is partly known. The paper now presents it as a quantified, controlled test of a community habit, not as a discovery that structured starts help. Comparisons with other numbers need care. MaCE's **~83%** soliton emergence is judged by eye in a mass-conserving variant, which is not comparable to our 7 to 23% verified gliders ([MaCE](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12306)).

**The Flow-Lenia sentence (A8)** was the clearest correction, and the evidence comes from our own repository. We checked the review's account against the journal and code, and it was too strong in one place.

- **E-16** ran 100 random rules × 4 starts: a soup at 0.35 occupancy, two mass-matched smooth fields (ℓ = 3 and 6), and **Chan's** multi-patch start (15 to 40 patches of side 12). Every rule had **one channel, R fixed at 13, and 1 to 3 kernels** (35, 37 and 28 of the 100 rules have 1, 2 and 3 kernels; `docs/data/rule-panel-e16.txt`, `FlowLenia.random` in `emergence/physics/flow_lenia.py`). The paper's centred patch was not one of E-16's starts; the E-16 plan says so ("Flow-Lenia's own paper uses one central patch").
- **J-016,** the earlier validation of our Flow-Lenia, did use the paper's start: **80 random rules in float precision from the paper's centred 40×40 uniform patch** (128×128, 500 steps), 20 with 3 kernels, 20 with 5, 20 with 10 (all at R = 13), and 20 with 10 kernels and R random in [8, 25]. It found 0 self-propelled structures. The 88 rules of a first, dropped uint8 version found none either; the [Report]({{ '/report.html' | relative_url }}) section 7 counts these as 168 random rules, 80 of them in float precision, from the paper's own patch.
- So J-018's phrase "from their own patch start or from any other" is accurate when J-016 and E-16 are read together. The review's notes called it wrong; that was too strong. J-018's "the paper's ranges, R = 13" is incomplete: the kernel ranges follow the paper, but the paper samples R from [2, 25].

Plantec et al. start from "a 40 × 40 patch with matter drawn from uniform distribution in the center of the grid", sample R from [2, 25], show 1- and 2-channel creatures and use 10 kernels in their timing benchmark ([Plantec et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07906)). The paper's "dynamic patterns are quite common" also covers "gyrating" and "snake like" motion, while our detector counts only isolated translating structures that survive alone. The 2025 journal version, read in full, still gives no counts: random search runs 150 steps from the 40×40 patch over 10⁵ parameter sets ([Plantec et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08569)). The 2023 paper's directed-motion optimisation found good solutions in 2 channels; single-channel runs reached similar fitness after about 5,000 generations, using 10 to 25 kernels.

The conclusion stands either way. The paper publishes no rate, our runs were single-channel with R fixed at 13 for most rules and few kernels, and our detector counts only isolated translating structures. So the result is **not comparable** with the paper. It is consistent with it, and it is not a failed reproduction ("could not reproduce" is withdrawn; D-066). A matched test (centred patch, R in [2, 25], about 10 kernels, 2 channels) has not been run.

**The multi-channel divider (A7)** needs framing. Chan 2020 says self-replication "turns out to be not rare in extended rules". Flow-Lenia 2023 calls "dividing and merging dots" "a common pattern" ([Chan 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03742); [Plantec et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07906)). Both were found by human-guided search or under different sampling. So our **1 in 1,000 random rules** does not contradict them, but the paper must quote them and explain the difference. Our diagnosis that offspring cannot inherit is already stated for MaCE: "The main missing piece towards evolution is the capacity to transmit their genes" ([MaCE](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.12306)). Our scrambled-lump test is a cleaner version of that point, not a new one.

**The related-work claim (M6)** that no Lenia work measures spontaneous emergence with nulls holds for every Lenia paper read: sensorimotor Lenia, Leniabreeder, ASAL, the glider equation, Particle Lenia, Flow-Lenia 2025 and the phase-boundaries paper. All of them search or optimise, and their only random baselines are rates per random *rule*. Hamon et al. found **30 moving stable solitons in 117,000 random-search trials, against 486 of 1,600 for IMGEP** ([Hamon et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12577699/)). Two non-Lenia preprints claim spontaneous self-replication in cellular automata and are now cited. Yin maps all 262,144 outer-totalistic Moore rules. Proliferation passes in 7.69% of rules, and the 1.56% "causal" rate is a chained estimate. Yin's validation calls Game of Life itself "positive for self-replication", and the detector's false-positive rate among negatives is **66% (40% at a stricter setting)** ([Yin 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25239v1)). Yin does use a single-cell deletion test in isolation, close in spirit to our knockouts. Hintze and Bohm study one novelty-searched, non-totalistic rule from one seed cluster, with no null ([Hintze and Bohm 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.08047v1)). Neither has a selection-aware null, so M6's methodological point survives in a narrower form.

**The Life-like results (A9)** fit census practice. Catagolue's census, read directly, holds **339,739,217,865,028** 16×16 soups at fixed size and density. From the census totals, Life yields about **1.92 gliders per soup**, so spontaneous Life movers are common ([Catagolue b3s23/C1](https://catagolue.hatsya.com/census/b3s23/C1); [glider census](https://catagolue.hatsya.com/textcensus/b3s23/C1/xq4)). A central-patch effect that vanishes at matched density is what a field that controls density by design would expect. Eppstein's rejection of random starts as a basis for classifying rules, and his menagerie of engineered patterns, are now cited for the "emergence, not existence" framing ([Eppstein 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.2890)). One small factual fix: Chan's code places `np.random.randint(15, 40)` patches, which is **15 to 39**, with values in [0, 0.9) and side int(0.9R) ([Chan's LeniaND.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Chakazul/Lenia/master/Python/LeniaND.py)). Our own `patch_mask` (`emergence/engine.py`) draws 15 to 40, one more possible value than Chan's code.

## After emergence: the negatives are what mechanism and theory predict

The source paper never claims adaptation, parasites or open-endedness for BFF. It calls its later dynamics anecdotal and says replicators "tend to remain broadly similar". So Finding C refutes nothing ([Agüera y Arcas et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19108)). **The heredity test (C1)** is our most solid measurement here. No systematic test of one-byte mutants in BFF was found, though heredity of variation is the baseline assumption in Avida and Tierra.

**"Later code is not fitter" (C4)** is in tension with the most widely cited digital result after emergence. In Tierra, Ray's 80-instruction ancestor, needing 839 CPU cycles per replication, evolved into a 22-instruction creature at 146 cycles: **a 5.75-fold efficiency gain** ([Ray, Tierra docs, node26](https://tomray.me/pubs/tierra/node26.html)). Hutton's Squirm3 shows selection "towards shorter molecules" and then nothing more. Pargellis reportedly saw cell size fall quickly after emergence (secondary only) ([Hutton 2002](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~turk/bio_sim/articles/hutton_rep_molecules.pdf)). The Google group's Z80 soups show replicator *mechanism* turnover, from Load–Push to LDIR, even without the task reward, only more slowly ([Cicala et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09211)). The usual published route to fitness gain after emergence is getting shorter, and BFF programs are fixed at 64 bytes. That is a mechanistic reason to expect no trend in BFF. It is an inference: no paper says it about BFF. The paper now says this rather than imply that emergent replicators in general do not improve. Lenski et al. 2003 strengthen the "expected" reading. Populations rewarded only for EQU evolved it in **0 of 50**, against **23 of 50** when simpler functions were also rewarded ([Lenski et al. 2003](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall24/cos597C/bib/598-2017/adami03Nature.pdf)). Reward-free soups sit at the far end of that gradient.

**"No parasites" (C6)** was not found as a BFF measurement. It is unusual against the wider literature. Tierran parasites "begin to emerge within the first few million instructions". They exploit template addressing and a memory "membrane" that "only protects write privileges" ([Ray, Tierra docs, node15](https://tomray.me/pubs/tierra/node15.html)). Stringmol parasites arise "almost immediately", and **12 of 20** spatial runs go extinct ([Hickinbotham et al. 2021](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/176868/1/rsos.210441.pdf)). Jha et al. report "parasitic stealing" in Z80 soups that have designed energy mechanics ([Jha et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10817), ABS). BFF lacks persistent bodies, protected genomes, template addressing and long-range reads. Its pairs are joined, run once and split, so a parasite has nothing to borrow. This makes the negative a statement about BFF's interaction geometry, and the paper now frames it that way.

**The space result (C7)** used the instrument the theory predicts is weakest. In spatial replicator theory, space mainly protects replicases against parasites. It can select for fast-rotating waves that hold *less* information, and it favours prudent traits that lose under mixing ([Takeuchi and Hogeweg 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3466355); [Kerr et al. 2006](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1038/nature04864%22&resultType=core&format=json)). Szabó et al.'s gains in efficiency and fidelity under limited dispersal came "despite the presence of molecular parasites" ([Szabó et al. 2002](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1038/nature01187%22&resultType=core&format=json), ABS). With no parasites and a well-mixed common-garden assay, E-78's null was the most likely outcome even if spatial adaptation had happened. Its point estimate (−0.42, lattice code slightly *less* fit when mixed) leans in the direction prudence predicts, though it is not significant. **The kin confound (C5)** is known in principle: assortment changes which strategy wins ([Takeuchi and Hogeweg 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2757730)). Its detection inside BFF invasion assays was not found elsewhere.

**Mutation regime.** The first pass suggested that BFF's sloppy copying puts it in the "survival of the flattest" regime. The gap note withdraws this. The 59-to-60-byte copiers are in variant rules. Emergent standard-BFF replicators copy with at least 90% identity in essentially every meeting in a halting background. Background mutation is only about **0.0154 per program per round**, far below Avida's 0.225 per replication in Lenski 2003 (J-050, J-051, `bff.py`, REPO). Wilke et al.'s result, that low-mutation dominants lost as mutation rose in 12 of 40 pairs, therefore cannot be invoked for standard BFF until the per-copy byte-error distribution is measured ([Wilke et al. 2001](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1038/35085569%22&resultType=core&format=json), ABS).

**"Level 5 not shown" (C8)** agrees with Bedau et al.'s view that "no existing artificial evolving system has class 3 dynamics". That was a suspicion drawn from Echo alone ([Bedau et al. 1998](https://people.reed.edu/~mab/papers/alife6.pdf)). Channon later claimed that Geb "pass[es] this test", so the consensus is not unanimous ([Channon 2001 record](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Channon_2001_PAT.html), ABS/SEC). More important, we have not computed the statistics the field uses. These are evolutionary activity against a neutral shadow, and the MODES change, novelty, complexity and ecology metrics on lineages that pass a persistence filter ([Dolson et al. 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10104628)). Level 5 has not been *tested* by field criteria. Only its precondition, directional adaptation, has been tested.

## Methods: standard practice made systematic, minus one standard correction

**Each ingredient of the validation regime (M2) has precedent.** Bedau's neutral shadow is a selection-off copy used to subtract non-adaptive activity. Bullock and Bedau tested whether activity statistics wrongly place unlifelike systems in class 3, which is the same idea as a deceptive control. Lohn and Reggia warned that automatically discovered self-replicating CA rules had to be checked by simulation ([Standish, nlin/0004026](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0004026); [Southampton eprint](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/261445/1/arl12201.pdf); [Lohn and Reggia](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/handle/1903/468)). Three things were not found elsewhere: a versioned probe suite with adversarial controls written for each detector, pre-registration in ALife experiments (one CA "intention manuscript" exists, [arXiv 2208.09444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09444)), and a public itemised table of a project's own detector failures. These are distinctive in practice, not in principle. **The ladder (M1)** assembles Bedau's classes, the MODES hierarchy and the OEE workshop's hallmarks, and we present it as an assembly ([Dolson et al. 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10104628); [Taylor et al. 2016](https://tim-taylor.com/paper-details/taylor2016openended.html)). **The rule-as-unit correction (M3)** is a textbook pseudoreplication fix. We first made the error, with p values 10⁵ to 10¹⁹ times too small, which argues against claiming methodological leadership.

**H1 (M4)** uses the standard design: guided against random search at equal budget, with independent seeds ([Reinke et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1908.06663)). Its **1.84×** is small next to Hamon et al.'s counts. Those imply roughly a thousandfold per-output advantage, a loose comparison because Hamon's random search also sampled initial patterns and its IMGEP used gradient refinement ([Hamon et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12577699/)). In Life-like space a ratio near 2 is unsurprising: Eppstein found spaceships in **10,736 of 16,384 B3 rules (65.5%)** ([Eppstein 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.2890)).

**The missing global correction** is the methodological gap that most needs fixing. The paper's §8 admits that the 45 pre-registered primaries were not corrected for multiple comparisons. Leniabreeder, a peer system, applies Holm–Bonferroni across its comparisons ([Faldor and Cully 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04235)). This change does not apply a correction: choosing the method and which p value counts as each primary is the owner's call (D-067, proposed).

*Rough check by the reviewing agent, not an analysis of ours:* a Bonferroni threshold of 0.05/45 ≈ 0.0011 would leave the world-level Lenia and BFF p values (10⁻⁹, 10⁻¹³, 10⁻²⁰) standing. E-75 phase 6 (p = 4 × 10⁻⁴) would also pass. It would not clear the rule-level sign tests (p = 0.016 and 0.031 in E-19a), the E-50 size exponent (p = 0.014) or the phase 7 rule-level p = 0.006 (which was computed after the fact, E-76; that phase's registered primary was undecided anyway). The headline claims that rest on rule-level evidence are exactly the ones a correction would weaken.

**AI-led authorship.** The paper's disclosure should cite the evidence that AI-scientist systems commit post-hoc selection and metric misuse that cannot be detected from the paper alone. An LLM classifier detected these failures at near-chance accuracy (51.4%) without trace logs ([Luo et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08713), ABS/SEC). Our committed journal and pre-registrations are the kind of trace that work recommends. The lack of any human domain-expert review stays a weakness, and it applies to this review too.

Our soups and lattices are the right size by the BFF source paper's standard. We are not "laptop-small" there, and the paper's "one laptop scale" undersells that point. Replication is the binding constraint everywhere. Emergence-rate estimates rest on about a hundredth as many runs as the source group uses, and the adaptation tests use about a seventh of Lenski's replicate count per condition. On compute, the BFF negatives are probably not a scale artefact. Tierran parasites appeared within a few million instructions, and 20,000 BFF rounds are many orders of magnitude more execution. That is an inference, and it should be checked against our step cap of 8,192 per pair. The instruments are the weaker axis: a well-mixed assay cannot see spatial adaptation, races and growth assays cannot see an advantage from mutational robustness, and no activity or MODES statistic was computed. On the detector side, our validated detectors are stricter than recent comparators. Yin's permissive proliferation screen has a 40 to 66% false-positive rate among negatives. Hamon's robustness-to-perturbation criterion for agency is stronger than our level 2.

## What this review did not cover

Several primary sources were closed or not reached, so the "not found in this search" labels stay provisional. The full texts of Coreworld (Rasmussen et al. 1990) and Pargellis 1996a, 1996b, 2001 and 2003 were not read: Elsevier, MIT Press and Wiley pages were closed, and core.ac.uk, direct.mit.edu and nokia.com returned 403. Whether those papers report emergence rates, waiting times or a dependence on population size is unknown, and that matters most for B2 and B10. Boerlijst and Hogeweg 1991, Channon's Geb papers, Fontana and Buss 1994 (the body is scanned images), Kawaguchi et al. 2021, Evans's Larger-than-Life, Pearson 1993, Typogenetics, "Capturing Emerging Complexity in Lenia" and Semantic Lenia were not read in full, or not at all. The Hamon et al. ablations were beyond the part of the text that was fetched. Langton, Wuensche and Zenil were read at abstract level at most, and the Agüera y Arcas books and the v1 against v2 differences of the source paper were not checked. That last check is needed before we claim the census explanation of reverse copying.

The search also skipped some areas entirely. Google Scholar was never queried, so the count of 29 citing papers comes from Semantic Scholar and may miss ALIFE proceedings. No 2020–2026 survey of open-ended evolution was found; the 2024 *Artificial Life* special-issue editorial returned 403. There was no dedicated search of Poisson or rare-event models of the origin of life in chemistry or theory, of quasispecies analyses of program soups, of phase-randomised surrogate starts in any CA, of ALife-specific discussions of pseudoreplication, of benchmark datasets for replicator detectors, of registered-report tracks, or of publisher policy on AI-led research. Several claims were not assessed against literature at all: wanderers, ring gliders, collisions and double gliders. On our side, the authors' Flow-Lenia reference code was not inspected for default channel and kernel counts, no matched Flow-Lenia test has been run (centred patch, R in [2, 25], about 10 kernels, 2 channels), and Yin's 20.8% causal pass rate was not re-derived. Many HTML sources were read through a summarising fetch tool, so short quotes are as that tool returned them.

## What it means for the project

The review moves the paper's centre of gravity. Its most defensible contributions are not "replicators emerge" or "Lenia makes gliders", which are both known. They are the *controlled counterfactuals* that the literature had treated as habit or anecdote: the shuffle-controlled correlation effect at matched mass, the single-change head-start counterfactual with a backward-copier control, a rule-level predictor tested prospectively across variant families, and heredity measured mutant by mutant. Each is "not found in this search", and each is strengthened by citing the work it sharpens. The negatives after emergence read better as a mechanistic diagnosis of BFF than as evidence about evolvability. BFF has fixed genome length, no persistent bodies, no protected genomes and no addressing at a distance, which removes the routes by which Tierra, Squirm3 and Stringmol produced shortening, parasites and arms races. That points to the next informative experiment: a variable-length or persistent-body BFF variant, or a lattice-native fitness assay. More 64-byte soups will not help.

Two pieces of housekeeping came before any outside reader sees the paper. The Flow-Lenia sentence turned a non-comparison into an apparent failed reproduction of a best-paper result; it is reworded in this change. A multiple-comparison correction across the 45 primaries will remove several rule-level passes on which the headline claims lean; it is still open (D-067).

**Open after this review:** the multiple-comparison correction (D-067); field-standard open-ended-evolution statistics (evolutionary activity against a neutral shadow, MODES); a lattice-native fitness assay; a matched Flow-Lenia test; the per-copy error rate of standard BFF replicators; and the closed sources listed above.
